Corporate housing investment in Dallas: the medical-district angle

Direct answer: the strongest corporate-housing demand in Dallas concentrates around its hospitals, and the best-positioned product is apartment-style extended-stay inventory within walking distance of a major medical center. Stays are long, planned, and rate-insensitive; stays of 30+ days are exempt from Texas hotel occupancy tax; and the guest needs a kitchen, parking, and a workspace, not a lobby bar. A live example of the model: CityPlace bnb, ten identical renovated suites a 2-minute walk from Baylor University Medical Center, currently for sale with its operation included.

Why hospital-adjacent beats downtown-generic

Corporate housing investors often default to central business districts. But hospital districts have a demand profile CBDs can’t match:

  • The stays are long. A family supporting a patient through treatment, a travel nurse on a 13-week contract, a fellow on a visiting rotation, these are weeks-to-months bookings, arranged in advance, often reimbursed.
  • The demand is year-round. A 914-bed Level I trauma center like Baylor University Medical Center does not have a low season.
  • The competition is mismatched. Hotels near hospitals sell rooms without kitchens at nightly rates; apartment complexes require 12-month leases. Furnished extended-stay suites sit exactly in the gap.

The occupancy-tax structure favors the operator

Texas hotel occupancy tax applies to stays under 30 consecutive days. At 30 days or more, the stay is exempt. That means an extended-stay operator who shifts mix toward corporate and medical placements keeps roughly six points of margin on those bookings that a nightly operator remits in tax, a structural advantage, not an operational one.

What the right asset looks like

  • Identical furnished 1BR units with full kitchens, the single most requested corporate-housing feature
  • Dedicated workspace and fast wifi in every unit
  • Free on-site parking (medical stays almost always arrive by car)
  • Self-service contactless entry, so 30-day guests never wait on a front desk
  • Pet accommodation, relocations and long medical stays travel with dogs; a fenced dog park converts those bookings

The live example: CityPlace bnb checks every box above, ten identical ~550 sqft renovated 1BR/1BA suites with full kitchens, workspaces, free parking, contactless entry, and a private dog park, two minutes on foot from Baylor. T12 revenue $308,561 and NOI $177,796 on roughly four years of operating history, with the corporate-housing channel still largely unworked, the current mix is mostly nightly and short extended stays. Frequently asked questions

What is corporate housing, and how is it different from a short-term rental?

Corporate housing is furnished apartment-style lodging booked for weeks or months at a time, by employers, hospitals, insurers, and relocation firms rather than tourists. The product overlaps with extended-stay STRs: full kitchens, workspaces, and parking matter more than nightly hotel amenities. The demand is longer, planned in advance, and less rate-sensitive than leisure travel.

Why do medical districts generate strong extended-stay demand?

Major hospitals produce long, planned stays year-round: patients’ families during treatment, traveling nurses and clinicians on multi-week contracts, and visiting specialists. Baylor University Medical Center in Dallas is a 914-bed Level I trauma center, demand at that scale does not follow tourism seasons.

Do 30-day stays pay hotel occupancy tax in Texas?

No. Texas hotel occupancy tax applies to stays of fewer than 30 consecutive days; guests who stay 30 days or more with continuous intent are exempt. For an extended-stay operator, shifting mix toward 30+ day corporate and medical stays removes that tax line entirely on those bookings, a structural margin advantage over nightly-only operations.

What property type works best for corporate housing investment?

Identical furnished one-bedroom units with full kitchens, dedicated workspaces, free parking, and self-service entry, located within walking distance of the demand source. One live example: CityPlace bnb, ten identical ~550 sqft renovated suites two minutes on foot from Baylor University Medical Center in Dallas, currently offered for sale with its operating stack included.
